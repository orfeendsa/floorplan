Notes for the architect

  1. Setbacks are 8′ front, 5′ rear, 5′ west side. Buildable envelope 29′ × 62′. The car porch roof covers the 5′ side strip plus a 4′ notch taken out of the building's front west corner, giving a 9′ × 16′ bay.
  2. All walls shown 9″. Bath, closet and light-well partitions could drop to 4½″ if non-load-bearing, recovering roughly 25 sq ft — worth checking against the structural grid.
  3. Dimensions on the drawing are wall centre to centre. The schedule gives clear internal sizes.
  4. The internal passage is 3′-3″ clear at 9″ walls. Confirm this is acceptable, or widen to 4′-0″ clear by taking 9″ from bedroom 1.
  5. The light well is open to the sky for the full height, with a louvered grille at roof level rather than glass, so it ventilates as well as lights. White plaster to all four faces, floor drain tied to the rainwater line.
  6. Courtyard and light well together give a stack path — cool air drawn in low at the rear courtyard, hot air out through the well. This is the main passive cooling strategy, since the east, west and south walls are all party walls.
  7. Stairs shown as a straight flight, 8′ × 12′ c/c. Confirm riser and going, and whether the well above stays open to the roof.
  8. Roof is the dominant heat load on a plot enclosed on three sides. Insulated slab or a roof cavity matters more here than wall orientation. Solar panels face south at roughly 28–30°; keep the stair reaching usable roof access.
  9. Verify whether the porch notch and the open shaft count against covered area. If exempt, the notch can be widened for an easier parking bay.
  10. First floor should not simply mirror this plan — upper west rooms lose the shading the neighbouring wall gives the ground floor.
